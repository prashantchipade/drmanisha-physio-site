Back & neck pain ＋

Back and neck pain commonly stem from issues in the musculoskeletal system, like muscle strains from poor posture or overuse, and degenerative conditions such as arthritis or herniated discs. Other causes include injury, inactivity, and in rare cases, infections or tumors.

Common Causes

Muscle Strain & Overuse: Repetitive motions, heavy lifting, or spending long hours in poor posture (like looking at screens) can strain muscles and ligaments supporting the spine.
Aging: Natural wear and tear can lead to degeneration of spinal joints, while the discs in the spine can weaken over time.
Injury: Accidents, such as whiplash from car crashes, can damage soft tissues, ligaments, discs, and joints.
Poor Posture: Hunching or holding your head forward for extended periods puts significant strain on the neck and spine.
Stress: Mental stress can cause people to tighten their neck and back muscles, leading to pain and stiffness.

How to avoid back & neck pain

Maintain Good Posture: Keep your head centered over your spine, with your ears directly above your shoulders and your shoulders aligned with your hips.
Working with Screens: Hold phones and tablets at eye level to avoid bending your neck down. Adjust your computer monitor to be level with your nose.
Ergonomic Setup: Use a supportive chair with armrests, and ensure your computer monitor and keyboard are positioned to maintain proper posture.
Maintain a Healthy Weight: Extra weight, especially around the stomach, can strain your lower back.
Stay Hydrated: Drink plenty of water to help keep the connective tissues and spinal discs hydrated and cushioned
Supportive Pillow: Use a pillow that keeps your head and neck aligned with your body
Avoid Stomach Sleeping: Try to avoid sleeping on your stomach, as it can strain your back.
Manage Stress: Practice relaxation techniques like belly breathing, as emotional stress can lead to muscle tension.

Sciatica, Spondylosis, Muscle Sprain

Knee pain ＋

Knee pain is often caused by injuries, medical conditions, or overuse, and can result from issues like ligament tears, arthritis, or tendonitis. Minor pain may respond to self-care, including rest, ice, compression, and elevation (RICE). For persistent or severe pain, or symptoms of injury, it's essential to consult a healthcare professional for proper diagnosis and treatment, which may involve physical therapy, bracing, or medication.

Common Causes

Injuries:

Ligament tears: Such as those to the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL).
Cartilage tears: A torn meniscus is a common example.
Tendonitis: Inflammation of the tendons, like patellar tendinitis, often from overuse.

Medical Conditions:

Arthritis: Includes osteoarthritis (cartilage wear) and rheumatoid arthritis (autoimmune condition).
Gout: Caused by uric acid crystals in the joint.
Bursitis: Inflammation of the fluid-filled sacs (bursae) cushioning the knee.

Overuse or Strain:

Repetitive stress on the knee joint from activities like running or jumping can lead to pain and problems.

Home Care for Minor Knee Pain

Rest: Avoid activities that worsen the pain.
Ice: Apply ice packs for 15-20 minutes every few hours to reduce pain and swelling.
Compression: Use an elastic bandage, strap, or sleeve to provide support and reduce swelling.
Elevation: Keep your knee elevated on a pillow when sitting or lying down.

How to avoid Knee Pain

Build Strong Muscles: Strengthen the quadriceps and hamstrings (muscles on the front and back of your thighs) to support your knee joints.
Improve Flexibility:: Regular stretching for your calves, hamstrings, and quadriceps helps maintain flexibility and prevent injury.
Incorporate Balance Training:: Exercises that improve balance help the muscles around your knees work together more effectively.
Be Smart About Exercise: If you experience chronic pain, consider switching to low-impact activities like swimming, water aerobics, or cycling.
Warm Up: Always warm up your muscles before exercising to prepare them for the demands of your activity.
Stay Hydrated: Dehydration can contribute to muscle aches and pains, so drink plenty of water throughout the day.
Choose Supportive Shoes: Wear supportive, well-cushioned shoes during physical activity to help take pressure off your knee joints and maintain proper alignment

Shoulder pain ＋

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt, and Could It Be Serious? Shoulder pain is a common symptom with many potential causes, including rotator cuff injuries, arthritis, bursitis, dislocations, and referred pain from the neck. Treatment for minor pain may include rest, ice or heat, over-the-counter pain relievers, and gentle movement. See a doctor for severe pain, persistent symptoms after few days, sudden pain, inability to move the arm, or signs of a dislocated or fractured bone.

Common Causes

Rotator Cuff Problems: Inflammation or tears in the tendons of the rotator cuff, the group of muscles that provide wide range of motion.
Arthritis: Degeneration of the cartilage in the shoulder joint.
Bursitis: Inflammation of the fluid-filled sacs that cushion the joint, often occurring with rotator cuff issues.
Frozen Shoulder: Stiffness in the shoulder joint that makes movement difficult and painful.
Shoulder Dislocation: The shoulder joint coming out of its socket.
Fractures: A broken bone in the shoulder or collarbone.
Impingement Syndrome: Tendons and bursae becoming trapped in the space under the "roof" of the shoulder, causing inflammation and pain.
Referred Pain: Pain that originates from another area, such as the neck or a spinal nerve problem, and is felt in the shoulder.

When to See a Doctor

You should seek medical advice for shoulder pain if you experience:

Sudden, severe pain. Inability to move your arm. A change in shape, significant swelling, or numbness in the arm or shoulder. Pins and needles or burning sensations. Pain that doesn't improve after two weeks of self-care.

Home Care for Minor Shoulder Pain

Rest: Avoid activities that worsen the pain.
Ice: Apply ice packs for 15-20 minutes every few hours to reduce pain and swelling.
Gentle Movement: Stay active and gently move your shoulder to prevent it from becoming stiffer.

How to avoid Shoulder Pain

Improve Posture: Sit and stand with your shoulders back, chest open, and avoid slouching, especially if you have a desk job.
Adjust Your Workspace: Set up your computer and workstation to support an ergonomic and healthy posture.
Take Breaks: Move around and take breaks during repetitive tasks or long periods of sitting to reduce strain on your shoulders.
Avoid Overloading: Don't carry heavy items that strain your shoulders, and try to distribute weight evenly rather than carrying bags on one shoulder.

Joint pain & arthritis ＋

Joint and arthritic pain refers to the pain and stiffness in joints caused by different forms of arthritis, which are conditions that damage the joint's cartilage, leading to bone-on-bone contact and inflammation. Symptoms include pain, swelling, loss of function, and morning stiffness that can range from minutes to hours. Treatment often involves medications, physical therapy, lifestyle changes like exercise and weight management, and sometimes surgery.

Common Symptoms:

Pain: Typically worse with activity and at the end of the day, though some forms of arthritis can cause pain at rest.
Stiffness: Particularly noticeable in the morning or after periods of rest.
Swelling: A sign of inflammation in the joint.
Reduced Range of Motion: Difficulty moving the joint.
Other Symptoms: Fatigue, weakness, and at times, issues with other organs like the skin or eyes

Common Types of Arthritis:

Osteoarthritis: The most common type, caused by wear-and-tear of joint cartilage over time.
Rheumatoid arthritis: An autoimmune condition where the immune system mistakenly attacks the joint lining, causing inflammation and potential joint destruction.
Gout: A form of arthritis caused by a buildup of uric acid in the body, leading to painful joint attacks, often in the big toe.
Psoriatic arthritis: Occurs in people with psoriasis and affects joints, often in the hands and feet.

Treatment and Management:

Exercise: Low-impact activities like walking, swimming, or bicycling can strengthen surrounding muscles and improve joint stability
Weight Management: Losing weight can significantly reduce the stress on weight-bearing joints.
At-Home Therapies: Applying heat (heating pads, warm baths) can relax muscles, while ice can reduce pain and swelling.
Physiotherapy: Different modelities in physiotherapys can help in reducing pain by reducing swelling and muscle stiffness.

Hip pain ＋

Hip pain can result from injuries, arthritis, structural problems, or other conditions like bursitis and sciatica, with pain in the groin or inside the hip suggesting a joint issue, and pain outside the hip indicating a soft tissue problem. For minor pain, self-care like rest, over-the-counter pain relievers, and applying heat or ice can help. However, you should see a physiotherapist for persistent pain.

Common Causes

Injuries: A fall or accident can cause a hip fracture, bruising, or damage to surrounding muscles, ligaments, and tendons.
Arthritis: Wear and tear on the cartilage can cause pain, stiffness (especially in the morning), and grating sounds with movement, often felt in the groin.
Bursitis: Inflammation of the bursa sacs, which cushion the hip joint, can cause pain.
Structural Issues: Conditions like hip instability or labral tears (damage to the cartilage rim) can lead to hip pain.
Referred Pain: Problems with the hip can sometimes be felt as pain in the knee.

What You Can Do:

Rest:: Avoid activities that aggravate the pain.
Apply Cold or Heat: Ice packs can reduce swelling, while warm baths or showers can relieve stiffness and prepare muscles for stretching.
Gentle Movement: Gentle stretching and appropriate exercises can help, but avoid overdoing it.
Physiotherapy: If pain persist, physiotherapy can help to heal the underlying injury and reduce pain completely

How to avoid Hip Pain:

Engage in Regular Exercise

Low-impact activities: Walking, swimming, cycling, and yoga are great options for keeping your hips mobile without excessive stress.
Strengthening exercises: Focus on exercises that strengthen the muscles around the hips, such as glute bridges, squats (modified if needed), and leg raises
Core strengthening: A strong core provides stability for your hips and back, reducing strain.
Avoid high-impact activities: If you're prone to hip pain, avoid activities like running or jumping that put a lot of pressure on the joint.

Foot & ankle pain ＋

Ankle and foot pain can result from injuries, such as sprains, fractures, and stress fractures, or chronic conditions like arthritis, plantar fasciitis, and tendonitis. Other causes include ill-fitting shoes, structural issues like flat feet, and nerve-related problems such as peripheral neuropathy or tarsal tunnel syndrome.

Common Causes

Injuries: Sprains, strains, fractures, and stress fractures from sports or accidents are common.
Arthritis: Osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis can cause pain and stiffness in the ankle and foot.
Tendonitis & Bursitis: Inflammation of the tendons (like Achilles tendonitis) or bursae (small fluid-filled sacs) can cause pain.
Plantar Fasciitis: Inflammation of the tissue on the sole of the foot, often causing heel pain.
Poorly Fitting Shoes: High heels or tight shoes can lead to soreness, bunions, and other foot problems.
Structural Issues: Conditions like flat feet (fallen arches) or bone spurs can cause pain.
Nerve Problems: Peripheral neuropathy or tarsal tunnel syndrome can cause burning, tingling, or numbness.

For minor pain or injuries:

Rest: Avoid activities that put weight on the affected foot or ankle.
Ice: Apply an ice pack (wrapped in a towel) for 15–20 minutes every few hours to reduce swelling.
Compression: Gently wrap the area with a bandage to provide support.
Elevation: Raise your ankle and foot to help reduce swelling.

How to avoid Foot & Ankle Pain:

Choose the right shoes: Select shoes that fit correctly and provide adequate arch support, cushioning, and space for your toes
Control your weight: Maintaining a healthy weight reduces the stress and pressure on your feet and ankles.
Strengthen and stretch:: Engage in regular exercises that strengthen the muscles in your feet and ankles and improve flexibility. Stretching the Achilles tendon and the plantar fascia can help prevent conditions like plantar fasciitis and Achilles tendonitis.

Spinal injuries ＋

A spinal cord injury is damage to the spinal cord—the bundle of nerves and nerve fibers that sends and receives signals from the brain. The spinal cord extends from the lower part of the brain down through the lower back. Direct injury to the spinal cord itself or damage to the tissue and bones (vertebrae) that surround the spinal cord can cause an injury. This damage can cause temporary or permanent changes in feeling, movement, strength, and body functions below the point where the injury happens.

Common Symptoms:

  • Numbness, tingling, or a loss of (or changes in) sensation in hands and feet
  • Paralysis (loss of movement)
  • Pain or pressure in the head, neck, or back
  • Weakness in any part of the body
  • Unnatural or uncomfortable positions of the spine or head
  • Loss of bladder and bowel control
  • Problems with walking
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Changes in sexual function

    • Types of Spinal Injuries:

    Cervical spine: This section is in your neck. It goes from the bottom of your skull to about the same level as your shoulders.
    Thoracic spine: This section stretches from your upper back to just below your navel (belly button).
    Lumbar spine: This section is in your lower back. It extends about to the top of where your buttocks meet, but your spinal cord ends a couple of inches above that.
    Sacral spine: This section is in your back. It contains nerve roots below your butt to your tailbone.

    Treatment and Management:

    Medications: Medications like steroids reduce swelling and inflammation around your spinal cord.
    Physiotherapy: Photobiomodulation is one of the promissing treatment option for healing the injury. This along with other modelities like traction helps hold you in place to minimize movement around your SCI.
    Splints, braces, halos or other support devices: Similar to traction, these keep the area around your SCI stable. But unlike traction, you can wear these and move around with them on.
    Surgery: The main priority of surgery is to relieve pressure on your spinal cord. It can also repair damage related to surrounding injuries pressing on your spinal cord

    Bell’s palsy ＋

    Bell palsy is an unexplained episode of facial muscle weakness or paralysis. It begins suddenly and can get worse over 48 hours. This condition results from damage to the facial nerve (the 7th cranial nerve). Pain and discomfort usually occur on one side of the face or head. The exact cause is unknown. Bell's palsy may be a reaction to a viral infection resulting in inflammation. It rarely occurs more than once. Bell's palsy is characterised by muscle weakness that causes one half of the face to droop. Physiotherapy can help prevent muscles from permanently contracting. Supportive steroids and antivirals can also be prescribed in some cases.

    Common Symptoms:

    The main sign of Bell’s palsy is facial muscle paralysis — usually on one side of your face. It looks like one side of your face is drooping.

  • Mild weakness to total paralysis on one side of the face — occurring within hours to days
  • Facial droop and trouble making facial expressions, such as closing an eye or smiling
  • Drooling
  • Pain around the jaw or pain in or behind the ear on the affected side
  • Increased sensitivity to sound on the affected side
  • Headache
  • Loss of taste
  • Changes in the amount of tears and saliva produced

    • Causes:

    Although the exact reason Bell's palsy occurs isn't clear, it's often related to having a viral infection. Viruses that have been linked to Bell's palsy include viruses that cause

  • Cold sores and genital herpes, also known as herpes simplex
  • Chickenpox and shingles, also known as herpes zoster
  • Infectious mononucleosis, caused by the Epstein-Barr virus
  • Cytomegalovirus infections
  • Respiratory illnesses, caused by adenoviruses
  • German measles, also known as rubella
  • Mumps, caused by the mumps virus
  • Flu, also known as influenza B
  • Hand-foot-and-mouth disease, caused by a coxsackievirus
    • Vertigo & balance ＋

    Vertigo is a sensation of spinning or whirling, often described as dizziness, where a person or their surroundings appear to be moving when they are not. It's commonly caused by issues with the inner ear or brain, which are responsible for balance. Vertigo can range from mild and brief to severe and debilitating, and it can be associated with other symptoms like nausea, vomiting, and difficulty walking.

    Common Symptoms:

    Besides the spinning sensation, individuals may experience: Nausea, Vomiting, Tinnitus (ringing in the ears), and Difficulties with balance and coordination.

    In simpler terms: Imagine you're on a merry-go-round, but you're not actually moving. That's essentially what vertigo feels like. It's a common condition with various potential causes, but often manageable with appropriate treatment.

    Common Causes:

    The main sign of Bell’s palsy is facial muscle paralysis — usually on one side of your face. It looks like one side of your face is drooping.

  • Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV), an inner ear problem
  • Inner ear infections
  • Meniere's disease
  • Migraines
  • Less frequently, brain tumors or strokes

    • Treatment and Management:

    Treatment depends on the cause and may involve medication, physical therapy (like canalith repositioning maneuvers for BPPV), or addressing the underlying condition.

    Postural correction & ergonomics ＋

    Postural correction involves achieving proper body alignment, while ergonomics is the science of designing your environment for comfort and efficiency, often to support good posture and prevent injury. To improve your posture, become mindful of your alignment, strengthen your core and back muscles with targeted exercises, and stretch tight muscles like your hip flexors and hamstrings. For ergonomics, adjust your workstation by ensuring your chair provides lumbar support and your monitor is at eye level, and remember to take regular breaks to move and stretch throughout the day.

    Benefits of good posture

    Pain Prevention:Poor posture and improper workstation setup can lead to musculoskeletal imbalances, muscle fatigue, and chronic pain.

    Injury Prevention:Correcting posture and practicing ergonomics reduces the risk of developing injuries, such as carpal tunnel syndrome or tendonitis.

    Improved Health:Maintaining proper alignment strengthens your spine and supporting muscles, enhancing overall balance, stability, and well-being.

    Increased Productivity: An ergonomic environment and good posture can boost efficiency and performance by reducing discomfort and strain.

    What to do to maintain good posture ?

    Be Mindful

    Regularly check and adjust your posture throughout the day, aligning your ears, shoulders, and hips.

    Strengthen Your Core

    Engage in exercises like planks and bridges to build a strong core that supports your spine.

    Stretch Tight Muscles

    Incorporate stretches for your hip flexors, hamstrings, and chest to release tension and improve alignment.

    Ergonomics

    Chair: Use a chair with lumbar support, ensuring your feet are flat on the floor and knees are level with your hips.

    Monitor: Position your computer screen at eye level to avoid bending your neck.

    Keyboard & Mouse: Place these within easy reach to prevent strain on your wrists and shoulders.

    Movement Breaks

    Take frequent short breaks to stand up, walk around, and stretch to improve circulation and prevent stiffness.

    Post-surgery rehabilitation ＋

    Post-surgery rehabilitation is a structured recovery plan involving therapeutic exercises, pain management, and education to restore strength, mobility, and overall function after a surgical procedure. Led by healthcare professionals like physical and occupational therapists, rehabilitation helps patients regain muscle strength, improve flexibility, prevent complications, and gradually return to daily activities, work, and sports safely.

    Goals of Post-Surgery Rehabilitation

    Restore Strength and Flexibility:Rebuilding muscle mass and loosening muscles to restore normal joint movement and function.

    Regain Mobility and Function:Helping the patient move smoothly and regain their ability to perform daily tasks and activities.

    Prevent Complications:Addressing issues such as stiffness, muscle weakness, and reducing the risk of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and respiratory problems.

    Manage Pain:Keeping pain levels under control so that patients can participate effectively in their rehabilitation program.

    Improve Mental and Emotional Well-being:Building confidence, providing emotional support, and giving patients a sense of accomplishment in their recovery journey.

    Importance of Following a Rehabilitation Plan

    Optimizing Recovery:Following a personalized plan ensures a safe, effective, and complete recovery, helping patients return to their pre-surgery levels of activity.

    Preventing Secondary Issues:Proper rehabilitation helps prevent complications like muscle atrophy, joint stiffness, and other functional limitations that can arise after surgery.

    Achieving Functional Independence:The structured approach helps patients gain the strength and confidence to live a fulfilling life, free from the limitations imposed by surgery.

    Sports injury management ＋

    Sports injury management involves an initial RICE protocol (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) to reduce pain and swelling, followed by seeking professional help from a doctor or physiotherapist for diagnosis and a personalized treatment plan. More serious injuries may require physical therapy, medication, or surgery, with a structured rehabilitation program to regain strength, flexibility, and psychological readiness before returning to sport.

    Immediate Treatment (RICE Protocol)

    Rest:Stop the activity to prevent further damage and allow the injured area to heal.

    Ice:Apply ice wrapped in a towel for 20 minutes every two hours for the first 48-72 hours to reduce swelling and pain. Do not apply ice directly to the skin.

    Compression:Use a firm elastic bandage to help reduce swelling, ensuring it's not too tight and doesn't cut off circulation.

    Elevation:Raise the injured area above the level of your heart to help reduce swelling.

    Professional Assessment and Further Management

    Consult a professional:For serious injuries or if symptoms persist, seek help from a doctor or physiotherapist for accurate diagnosis and treatment. Physical Therapy:A physiotherapist can provide exercises to help regain flexibility and strength and to return to normal activities safely. Medications:Pain relievers or anti-inflammatory drugs may be recommended to manage pain and swelling. Immobilization:In some cases, a cast or splint may be necessary to keep the injured tissues in place for proper healing. Surgery:For severe injuries like fractures or ACL tears, surgical intervention may be required. Rehabilitation and Return to Sport Structured Rehabilitation:Follow a rehabilitation plan that addresses physical strength, flexibility, and sport-specific movements. Psychological readiness:Acknowledge the importance of mental health in recovery, providing support to help athletes manage the psychological impact of the injury. Gradual return to play:A partial return to activity, rather than a sudden increase in workload, can prevent re-injury. Functional testing:Assess the athlete's physical and psychological readiness through sport-specific tests before they return to their sport.

    Geriatric physiotherapy ＋

    Geriatric physiotherapy is a specialized field focusing on older adults, addressing age-related conditions like reduced mobility, muscle weakness, balance problems, and chronic pain. It involves personalized treatment plans with exercises to improve strength, flexibility, and balance, manual therapy for pain, and strategies for fall prevention. The primary goals are to enhance mobility, maintain independence, and improve the overall quality of life for seniors

    Common conditions treated

  • Arthritis, osteoporosis, and other musculoskeletal problems.
  • Balance disorders and reduced coordination, which increase the risk of falls.
  • Muscle weakness, reduced flexibility, and stiffness.
  • Chronic pain and the recovery process after surgery, stroke, or injury.
  • Neurological conditions like Parkinson's disease

    • Benefits for seniors

    Improved Mobility:Helps individuals move more freely and perform daily activities like walking and climbing stairs.

    Enhanced Independence:Supports the ability to perform everyday tasks without assistance, boosting confidence.

    Pain Management:Reduces chronic pain and discomfort associated with age-related conditions.

    Fall Prevention:Addresses balance issues and strength deficits to mitigate the risk of falls, which can have severe consequences for older adults.

    Better Quality of Life:Contributes to overall well-being and helps seniors remain active and engaged.

    Advanced physiotherapy

    Technology combined with clinical judgement.

    Therapeutic modalities may be included when appropriate as part of a broader rehabilitation plan—not as a substitute for assessment or active rehabilitation.

    Photo-biomodulation by Class-IV Laser

    Photobiomodulation (PBM) with a Class IV laser uses high-intensity light to stimulate cellular activity, promoting healing and pain reduction by increasing ATP production, reducing inflammation, and accelerating tissue repair. Class IV lasers, unlike surgical lasers, are designed for therapeutic use, delivering a large amount of photonic energy in a shorter time to penetrate tissues deeply. This non-thermal process interacts with chromophores in cells, triggering a cascade of beneficial physiological reactions.

    Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy (ESWT)

    Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy (ESWT) uses focused or radial acoustic pressure waves to stimulate the body’s natural healing response in painful or poorly healing tissues. The mechanical energy delivered into the affected area can improve local circulation, influence pain pathways, stimulate cellular activity, and promote tissue remodeling. Shockwaves may also help break down pathological calcifications in certain conditions, such as calcific tendinopathy. Unlike surgical shockwaves used for stone fragmentation, therapeutic shockwave systems are designed to deliver controlled mechanical energy to musculoskeletal tissues. This non-invasive treatment is commonly used for conditions such as chronic tendinopathies, plantar fasciitis, tennis elbow, and other persistent soft-tissue disorders.

    Combination Therapy ( Laser | IFT | Traction )

    Laser combination therapy with Interferential Therapy(IFT) & Decompression Therapy (Traction) involves using these three modalities together in physiotherapy for comprehensive pain relief, enhanced healing, and accelerated recovery. This combined approach leverages IFT's deep tissue electrical stimulation for muscle and nerve stimulation, Decompression Therapy (Traction) for releasing inter-bone compression with the biostimulatory effects of laser therapy to promote cellular repair and reduce inflammation. The synergy of these treatments is typically delivered through advanced, multi-modality devices designed for both clinical use.

    Combination Therapy ( Laser | IFT | Shockwave )

    Shockwave + Class-IV Laser Therapy combines mechanical acoustic energy with high-intensity photobiomodulation to support healing, reduce pain, and improve tissue function. Shockwaves provide controlled mechanical stimulation that promotes circulation, tissue remodeling, and the body’s natural repair response, while Class-IV laser therapy delivers photonic energy to stimulate cellular activity, support ATP production, and help modulate inflammation and pain. Used together, these complementary therapies can target both the mechanical and cellular aspects of tissue recovery, making the combination extremely effective in many chronic musculoskeletal and soft-tissue conditions..

    Combination Therapy ( US | IFT | Traction )

    Ultrasound Combination Therapy with Interferential Therapy (IFT) & Decompression Therapy (Traction) is a physiotherapy technique that simultaneously applies high-frequency sound waves (ultrasound), low-frequency electrical currents (IFT) & Decompression Therapy (Traction) for releasing inter-bone compression to the same area to promote pain relief, reduce inflammation, and accelerate healing through a synergistic effect.

    Patient information

    Frequently asked questions.

    What is physiotherapy? ＋

    Physiotherapy helps restore movement and function when affected by injury.

    Do I need a doctor’s referral? ＋

    No, you can book directly without a referral in most cases.

    What should I bring to my appointment? ＋

    Bring previous reports, X-rays, MRI and any prescriptions.

    What conditions can a physiotherapist treat? ＋

    Musculoskeletal : Neck and back pain, arthritis, joint pain, ligament and bone issues, and post-trauma stiffness. Neurological : Conditions like stroke, Parkinson's disease, and multiple sclerosis to improve function and mobility. Other :Issues arising from various injuries or surgeries.

    Will my condition relapse after taking Class IV Laser treatment? ＋

    Laser therapy, especially Class IV, is a boon for physiotherapy as its light energy can penetrate more than 5 cm into the body to heal deep-seated injuries—an alternative that previously required surgery. Such injuries often result from factors like excess weight, nutritional deficiencies, poor lifestyle, or faulty ergonomics. However, unless these underlying issues are corrected after healing, there is a high risk of re-injury at the same site. Physiotherapy sessions—whether Laser, Ultrasound, IFT, or any other modality—are designed to help you recover from injury and break the cycle of pain. It’s much like taking antibiotics for an infection: antibiotics help your body fight off the infection, but once the course is complete, it’s up to you to maintain hygiene, maintain healthy lifestyle to prevent reinfection.

    What can I expect during a session? ＋

    Initial Assessment : A thorough examination of your medical history, symptoms, and goals. Treatment Plan : A personalized plan with specific interventions. Interventions : May include therapeutic exercises, manual therapy, laser therapy, ultrasound therapy, electrotherapy or decompression therapy to manage pain and inflammation, and education on posture and ergonomics. Progress Monitoring : Your progress is continuously monitored, and the treatment plan is adjusted as needed.

    What should I do to prepare for a session? ＋

    Clothing : Wear comfortable, loose-fitting clothing that allows for easy movement, and athletic shoes if exercises are involved. Documents : Bring any relevant medical reports and a list of your symptoms and goals for the session.

    What are common concerns about physiotherapy? ＋

    Pain : Physiotherapy should not be painful; while some mild discomfort might occur, you should communicate any pain to your physiotherapist so they can adjust the treatment. Duration : Physiotherapy is often an ongoing, comprehensive process, not a one-time treatment, with sessions typically lasting 30-60 minutes, though the initial assessment can be longer.

    How long will physiotherapy take to work? ＋

    The total duration of treatment depends on the specific condition, its severity, and how consistently you follow the plan. While some people notice improvements within a few sessions, others with more complex or chronic issues may need several weeks or months of consistent therapy

    What are the benefits of physiotherapy? ＋

    Key benefits of physiotherapy include: Pain relief : Helps reduce chronic and acute pain without relying heavily on medication. Improved mobility : Increases flexibility and range of motion, making daily activities easier. Enhanced balance : Strengthens muscles and improves stability, reducing the risk of falls. Injury prevention : Teaches proper techniques and body mechanics to avoid future injuries. Non-invasive approach : Offers an alternative to surgery for many conditions

    Do you offer home visits? ＋

    No

    What is meant by treatment plan ? ＋

    The treatment plan is a blend of Laser and Electro therapy, designed to effectively alleviate pain and expedite recovery. Each individual’s condition necessitates a tailored treatment strategy. Factors such as age, type of injury, overall health, and the physical demands of your occupation all play a role in shaping the treatment plan for effective and long-term recovery. The duration of each therapy is carefully calibrated to deliver optimal doses for maximum healing. Each treatment plan is grounded in extensive experience dealing with various conditions and understanding how your body responds to each therapy.

    Why is it crucial to consistently follow the doctor’s recommended sessions without any breaks? ＋

    The human body requires a specific duration for ideal recovery. The initial sessions are structured to alleviate pain and break the cycle of discomfort. The following sessions support the body’s healing process, speeding up recuperation and fostering complete recovery. If there are disruptions, the body might revert to its defensive mechanism, possibly leading you back into the cycle of pain. Hence, it is extremely important to stick to the sessions as prescribed by the doctor.

    In the course of physiotherapy, will the treatment plan solely consist of exercises, or will it also include other forms of treatment or medication? ＋

    Within the scope of physiotherapy, subsequent to a comprehensive problem assessment, recommendations may encompass either a regimen of exercises or the utilization of physical therapies. Among the physical therapies available, we offer both electrotherapy and advanced photobiomodulation therapy. These modalities are designed to effectively address the underlying causes of issues such as muscle injuries, strains, and spasms. Following these therapy sessions, a customized exercise plan will be prescribed to mitigate the risk of recurrence and to foster enduring resolution of the concern. We prioritize minimizing the use of medication whenever feasible, as it generally offers only transient relief.

    What are the various therapies included in physiotherapy treatment? ＋

    There are two very effective line of therapies –Laser therapy and Electro thereapy. Laser Therapy – It is most advance treatment method. Class IV Laser Therapy is non-invasive use of laser energy to generate a Photochemical response in damaged or dysfunctional tissues. Laser Therapy can alleviate pain, reduce inflammation, and accelerate recovery at a very fast pace. Class IV laser therapy stimulates and treats the root cause of pain, resulting in faster and long-lasting pain relief. Electro Therapy – This is combination of IFT and Ultrasound therapy. This helps in reducing muscle spasm and reduction in pain

    Is Laser Therapy Safe? ＋

    Class IV laser therapy is extremely safe and all that is usually felt is a mild warming sensation. Plus safety glasses and all safety considerations are in place before therapy starts.
